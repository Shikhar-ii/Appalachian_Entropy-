
# Signal, Entropy, and Regime Persistence
### Quantitative Inference of Hidden Economic Structure Across Appalachia

## Research Overview

This research investigates the underlying economic and demographic
structures of 423 Appalachian counties across 13 U.S. states through
mathematical modeling, statistical inference, and multivariate analysis.

The study integrates historical population dynamics, socioeconomic
indicators, labor-market characteristics, and regional economic
heterogeneity using official government statistics.

## Mathematical and Analytical Framework

- Statistical inference and econometric modeling
- Multivariate regression analysis
- Longitudinal and time-series analysis
- Principal Component Analysis (PCA)
- Unsupervised learning and K-means clustering
- Correlation and covariance analysis
- Demographic trajectory classification
- Socioeconomic structural heterogeneity

The broader theoretical framework incorporates:

- Shannon entropy and information theory
- Economic complexity and industrial diversification
- Graph theory and spatial economic relationships
- Economic regime transitions and persistence
- Regional resilience and structural dynamics

Entropy estimation, network modeling, and regime-transition analysis
represent extensions of the current empirical research.

## Research Objectives

1. Identify underlying socioeconomic structures across Appalachian counties.
2. Quantify demographic and economic variation over time.
3. Estimate relationships between workforce characteristics, education,
   population, and household income.
4. Identify county-level socioeconomic patterns through dimensionality
   reduction and clustering.
5. Develop a mathematical framework for investigating economic entropy,
   structural transitions, and regime persistence.

## Data Sources
